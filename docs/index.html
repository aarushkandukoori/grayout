Questions, answered

Billing questions

Grayout asks an AI model one question about your screen: is this person clearly not working? Add a one-line description of your work in Settings and it judges against that. Anything ambiguous counts as work.

No. When a video-call app such as Zoom, FaceTime, Teams or Webex is in front, Grayout skips the check and clears any alert. You can change that list in Settings.

Each display is resized and sent for a verdict, then dropped. On the free checks and Pro it passes through the Grayout service to OpenAI’s model, and the service never stores or logs it. In today’s 1.0.1 release, which runs on your own key, it goes straight to OpenAI or Anthropic under your account. When a password manager is in front, nothing is captured at all.

Get back to work and the next check restores it. You can also click I’m working, pause, or choose Restore color now in the menu bar. Quitting restores color, and a 20-minute watchdog does too.

About 2 hours of active use at the 45-second default. A check is only spent when you’re at your Mac and something on screen changed. That’s enough to watch it catch you. No card, no account, no email.

The free checks start when the Grayout service opens. Until then, today’s release runs on your own OpenAI or Anthropic key.

Yes, with room to spare. A full month of work at the default pace is around 8,000 checks before unchanged screens are skipped, and roughly half that after. If you ever pass 10,000, Grayout keeps going at one check every 10 minutes until the month resets.

Grayout isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so the first launch needs Open Anyway in System Settings. The install guide walks through it in three clicks.
