Privacy

Grayout looks at your screen, so this page is specific about where every byte goes. The same text ships in the repository as PRIVACY.md.

Effective 2026-10-15. Covers this website and Grayout for Mac however you got it (GitHub, Homebrew or the Mac App Store): Grayout 2.0 and later, which run on the Grayout service (the 100 free checks, the trial and Grayout Pro), and Grayout 1.x, which runs on your own API key.

The short version

  • Grayout asks first (2.0 and later). Before any screenshot leaves your Mac, the app shows what it sends at each check and who receives it, and asks for your permission. Until you click Allow and continue, it captures nothing and sends no screenshot, app name, work description or camera frame, and you can withdraw it any time in Settings. Grayout 1.0.x, which runs on your own API key, has no such step (see Asking first).
  • Screenshots are judged and dropped. Each check sends a resized screenshot for a one-line verdict. Nothing in the chain we run stores it or logs it.
  • With the Grayout service (Grayout 2.0 and later, in either edition: the free checks, the trial and Grayout Pro), the screenshot goes to the Grayout service, which passes it to OpenAI under Grayout’s key and returns the verdict. OpenAI doesn’t train on it and may keep it for up to 30 days to check for abuse. The service keeps device and license records and counts of checks. It does not keep the image.
  • On your own key (Grayout 1.x only; 2.0 and later have no own-key mode), the screenshot goes straight from your Mac to OpenAI or Anthropic under your account. We are not in the path at all.
  • Payments are handled by Stripe and sold through Link. Grayout never sees your card number and never stores your email address. To give one free trial per person, the service reads your email and your card’s fingerprint from Stripe when a trial starts and keeps only keyed hashes of them.
  • This website sets no cookies and runs no analytics.

Asking first

From Grayout 2.0, the first time Grayout starts, and once after updating from 1.0.x (which did not ask), it shows a screen before anything is captured. That screen says:

  • what is sent at each check: a screenshot of each display, resized; the name of the app in front; your work description and task titles if you add them; and one webcam frame, only if you turn the camera on;
  • who receives it: OpenAI, through the Grayout service;
  • that the Grayout service keeps none of it.

Not now means nothing is captured and no check is sent, and setup can’t finish. Allow and continue records your permission, with the date, on your Mac only. To withdraw it, turn off Send screenshots for checks in Settings: checks stop at once, and no check is sent until you turn it back on. If what Grayout sends or who receives it ever changes, the app asks again.

Before you allow it, and while that switch is off, Grayout sends no screenshot, app name, work description or camera frame. It may still contact the Grayout service to register this Mac or check your plan, with the identifiers listed below, and the direct download may ask GitHub for the latest version number unless you switched that off.

Grayout 1.0.x runs on your own API key and has no consent step, no Send screenshots for checks switch and no erase control. Its first screen says what leaves your Mac, Pause or Quit stops checks, and Delete all history in Settings empties the log.

The prompt asks the model for three fields: whether you are off task, a short activity phrase, and a confidence. It tells the model to name only a category of what it sees, such as “code editor and terminal,” and never on-screen text, names, message contents, links or personal details. Task titles and your work description are marked as untrusted, and the model is told to ignore any instructions inside them.

Screenshot files are written to a private temporary folder and deleted right after they are encoded. Webcam frames never touch the disk.

The camera is used only for the optional webcam check. macOS asks for permission the first time you turn it on. Turn it off in Settings, or revoke the permission any time in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Camera, and no frame is taken. The Mac App Store edition asks for the camera only for this option.

What the Grayout service stores

This applies to Grayout Pro and the 100-check free taste, in either edition, once the service opens. Not stored, anywhere in the service: screenshots, webcam frames, prompts, verdicts, activity phrases, your work description, task titles, names, email addresses, card details, IP addresses in clear, or license keys. They exist for the length of one request and are dropped.

What is stored, in a Cloudflare D1 database:

  • For each device (a Mac running Grayout, or a browser that clicked Subscribe): a random id, when it was created and last seen, how many free checks it used, whether it started a trial, and which license it uses. For 48 hours only, a keyed hash of its IP address, used only to limit how many devices one network can create. The rest of the device record has no end date yet.
  • For each license: a one-way hash of the key, the Stripe customer id, the subscription state copied from Stripe (status, plan, period dates), and monthly check counts. The record is kept while the license exists, and each month’s count for 13 months.
  • For each network, free checks only: a keyed hash of the network address and how many free checks it used this month, deleted when the month ends. On IPv6, also a keyed hash of its /48 block (a wider address range that can hold many networks) and how many free checks the block used each day, deleted after 7 days. This is what stops one network from using up everyone’s free checks.
  • For each trial, for one year: keyed hashes of the buyer’s email address and card fingerprint, only to give one free trial per person. Deleted after a year. A keyed hash can’t be turned back into the email or the card.
  • For each checkout: the Stripe Checkout Session id, the device that started it, the plan, whether it came with a trial, and whether it was paid. Deleted after 30 days. A purchase started in the app also has a short code that links it to that Mac, deleted a day after the code expires.
  • Webhook bookkeeping: the ids of Stripe events already handled, so none is processed twice. Deleted after 30 days.

Every keyed hash here is an HMAC made with a secret that only the service holds.

Requests are also rate limited by network address. Those limits count requests in memory and write nothing down. Cloudflare runs the service’s infrastructure and sees each connection the way any host does; see Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

The AI model, and who else handles your data

With the Grayout service, the service sends the screenshot and the rest of the check to OpenAI’s API under Grayout’s API key, with no identifier of yours attached, and asks OpenAI not to store the response. OpenAI does not use data sent through its API to train its models. Under its API data policy it may keep API inputs, which here include the images, for up to 30 days to monitor for abuse, and then deletes them unless the law requires it to keep them longer. See OpenAI’s enterprise privacy commitments, which cover its API, and how OpenAI handles API data.

In Grayout 1.x, which runs on your own key, your Mac sends the screenshot to OpenAI or Anthropic under your account, and your own terms with that provider apply: see the OpenAI pages above or Anthropic’s privacy policy. Grayout 2.0 and later have no own-key mode.

Apart from the provider whose key you use in Grayout 1.x, the app’s data goes to three service providers only: OpenAI (the AI model), Cloudflare (which runs the Grayout service) and Stripe (payments, through Link). We only use service providers that are bound by written data-protection terms giving your data the same or equal protection as this policy. Each one gets only what its part needs and may use it only to provide that service to us. Stripe, as the merchant of record, also handles your payment details under its own privacy policy. We don’t sell your data and we don’t share it for advertising.

Payments

Grayout Pro is sold through Link, Stripe’s checkout, and Stripe is the merchant of record. When you subscribe, you are on Stripe’s page under Stripe’s terms. Stripe collects your email, card and the billing address it needs for tax, calculates and collects sales tax or VAT, sends receipts from Link, and answers payment questions. Your statement shows LINK.COM*.

Grayout never sees your card number and never stores your email address. From Stripe, the service learns a customer id, a subscription id, the plan and its status. Stripe’s record of a purchase also carries your email address, and when a trial starts the service reads your card’s fingerprint (Stripe’s id for a card, which can’t be used to charge it). It uses the two only to make the keyed hashes described above, at once, and keeps neither. See Stripe’s privacy policy and Link’s privacy policy.

This website

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which sees your IP address and browser details when you load a page; see GitHub’s privacy statement. Fonts and scripts are served from the same place. There are no cookies, no analytics, no ads and no third-party scripts.

Once subscriptions open, the site talks to the Grayout service in four cases only: a status check for the footer, registering this browser when you click a subscribe button (kept in your browser’s local storage as grayout.device), fetching your key on the page Stripe returns you to (kept for that tab in session storage as grayout.claim), and opening the billing portal if you paste a license key on the account page. Clearing your browser’s site data removes both.

A link to the Mac App Store, where the site shows one, is a plain link served from this site. Nothing loads from Apple until you click it, and on apps.apple.com Apple’s privacy policy applies.

If you got Grayout from the Mac App Store

Apple distributes and reviews this edition, but Apple is not a party to this policy, and Grayout sends Apple nothing about your checks. Apple’s own privacy policy covers your download and anything you choose to share with developers through Apple, such as crash reports.

  • It asks first, sends the same things to the same places, and the Grayout service keeps the same records, all as described above.
  • It always uses the Grayout service, and it does not read Canvas or a task file.
  • Grayout Pro is still bought on Stripe’s checkout page in your browser, not through Apple, so Apple doesn’t see or handle that payment.
  • It gets updates from the App Store and never contacts GitHub.
  • It runs in Apple’s App Sandbox and keeps its files in ~/Library/Containers/com.aarushkandukoori.grayout/Data/Library/Application Support/Grayout/.

What stays on your Mac

Everything lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Grayout/ (in the Mac App Store edition, the container folder above), readable only by your user account.

  • config.json: your settings, including the work description.
  • secrets.bin: your license key (in Grayout 1.x, your API key), your device token, and any Canvas token, encrypted with a key held in your macOS Keychain. There is no plain-text copy.
  • verdicts.jsonl: one line per check with the time, the verdict, the category phrase, the app in front and the number of displays. No screenshots and no on-screen text. Kept for 30 days and deletable any time from Settings. This file never leaves your Mac.
  • state.json: your device id, setup progress, whether and when you allowed checks to be sent (from 2.0), and update-check bookkeeping.
  • logs/grayout.log: short status lines, capped at a few megabytes. Never images, prompts, task titles, your work description or any key.

Other people on your screen

A screenshot can include other people: a call, a shared screen, a chat. Two defaults reduce that, and both are yours to change. When a video-call app such as Zoom, FaceTime, Teams or Webex is in front, no check happens. When a password manager such as 1Password, Bitwarden or Passwords is in front, nothing is captured at all. Both rules look at the app in front only, so a window left open behind it can still appear in a later capture. Pause Grayout when that matters.

Your controls

  • Send screenshots for checks in Settings (2.0 and later). Off withdraws your permission and stops all checks until you turn it back on.
  • Pause for 15 minutes, an hour, until tomorrow, or until you resume. Nothing is captured while paused.
  • I’m working clears the gray and holds off for ten minutes.
  • Never capture and never trigger lists in Settings.
  • The camera is off unless you turn it on, and macOS lets you revoke it in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Camera.
  • Delete all history in Settings empties the log.
  • Erase this Mac’s Grayout data in Settings (2.0 and later) deletes Grayout’s settings, history, license key and device token on this Mac and restarts it fresh. It does not cancel a subscription or delete what the service holds; see below.
  • Quit stops everything and restores color.
  • Cancel any time from Manage subscription or at link.com. A lapsed plan stops the checks. It does not gray your screen.

Retention and deletion

  • Screenshots and webcam frames: not kept by us. OpenAI (with the Grayout service) may keep them for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring, as described above. In Grayout 1.x, on your own key, your provider handles them under its own policy.
  • Device records: kept with no end date (there is no automatic deletion yet). They hold a random id and counters, nothing that names you, and the IP address hash in them is cleared after 48 hours.
  • License records: kept while the license exists. Monthly check counts: 13 months.
  • Free-check counts per network: until the month ends. Per IPv6 /48 block: 7 days. Trial hashes: one year. Checkout records and webhook ids: 30 days. App purchase codes: a day after they expire.
  • Stripe’s records: kept by Stripe as its terms and tax law require. We can’t delete an invoice.
  • Everything on your Mac: yours. It is gone when you delete the folder above or, from 2.0, use Erase this Mac’s Grayout data in Settings.

To have your license record deleted, ask Link to delete your data at link.com. Stripe then cancels the subscription and tells us, and we delete your license record, its monthly check counts and your trial hashes. Your device records are unlinked from it and kept, holding only a random id and counters. Or email from the address you paid with.Please don’t post your email address or license key in a public GitHub issue.

Not for watching other people

Grayout is built for one person watching their own screen. It has no remote view, no team plan and no way to share what it saw, and it never will. Don’t install it on a computer you don’t own or on someone else’s account.

Children

Grayout is not directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect personal information from them. You must be at least 13 to use Grayout, and anyone under 18 needs a parent or guardian’s permission (see the terms). If you think a child under 13 has used the Grayout service, contact us and we will delete what the service holds about that install.

Why macOS warns you about the direct download

The direct download is signed ad hoc and not yet notarized by Apple, so its first launch shows a warning. Every release is built by GitHub Actions from a tagged commit in the public repository, with SHA-256 checksums on the release page. Apps from the Mac App Store are signed by Apple and open without this warning.

Changes and contact

Changes are dated at the top of this page and recorded in the repository’s history. Anything that sends something new off your Mac, or stores something new, is called out in the release notes. If what the app sends or who receives it changes, Grayout 2.0 and later ask for your permission again.

Questions: or GitHub issues, or see support. Security reports: the repository’s private vulnerability reporting, described in SECURITY.md.
